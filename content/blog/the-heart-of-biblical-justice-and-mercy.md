# The Heart of Biblical Justice and Mercy

## A Bible Study on Exodus 22:21-25

**Scripture Focus: Exodus 22:21-25**

> “You shall neither mistreat a stranger nor oppress him, for you were strangers in the land of Egypt. You shall not afflict any widow or fatherless child. If you afflict them in any way, and they cry at all to Me, I will surely hear their cry; and My wrath will become hot, and I will kill you with the sword; your wives shall be widows, and your children fatherless. If you lend money to any of My people who are poor among you, you shall not be like a moneylender to him; you shall not charge him interest.” (NKJV)

### Introduction

The laws found in Exodus are not merely regulations for an ancient nation. They reveal the character of God and His concern for how people treat one another. In Exodus 22:21‑25, God focuses on three groups who were especially vulnerable in society:

- **Strangers** (foreigners and outsiders)
- **Widows**
- **Orphans**

God’s instructions demonstrate a fundamental principle that runs throughout Scripture: the strength of a society is revealed by how it treats those who have the least power.

These verses challenge believers today to examine their attitudes toward the marginalized, the vulnerable, and those who cannot repay kindness with influence or reward.

---

### God Remembers the Stranger
> “You shall neither mistreat a stranger nor oppress him..."

Israel was commanded to remember its history. Before becoming a nation, the Israelites lived as foreigners in Egypt. They knew what it felt like to be overlooked, marginalized, and mistreated. God wanted His people to remember their past so they would extend compassion to others.

**Application Today:** When believers forget where God brought them from, pride often replaces compassion. We begin to judge others harshly rather than remembering the grace we ourselves received. God calls His people to welcome the outsider, not because that person has earned acceptance, but because God’s people have already received mercy.

#### Reflection
- Do I treat newcomers with kindness and patience?
- Have I forgotten what it was like when I needed God’s grace?
- How can I become more welcoming to those who feel like outsiders?

---

### God’s Protection of the Vulnerable
> “You shall not afflict any widow or fatherless child."

In ancient society, widows and orphans often lacked financial security and legal protection. Without a husband or father, many became easy targets for exploitation.

God specifically identifies Himself as their defender. Throughout Scripture, God’s heart is repeatedly shown toward the vulnerable:
- He defends the widow.
- He cares for the orphan.
- He protects the poor.
- He hears the cries of the oppressed.

**Intensity:** God does not simply recommend kindness. He warns that mistreatment of the vulnerable will provoke His judgment. The Lord takes injustice personally.

#### Reflection
- Are there vulnerable people around me whom I have overlooked?
- Do my actions reflect God’s compassion?
- How can I become a source of support rather than indifference?

---

### God Hears the Cry of the Oppressed
> “I will surely hear their cry."

These words provide comfort to anyone who has suffered injustice. Human institutions sometimes fail. Leaders sometimes fail. Friends sometimes fail. But God never fails to hear.

**Biblical Examples:**
- Israel cried out in Egypt.
- Hannah cried out in her barrenness.
- David cried out in distress.
- The blind cried out to Jesus.

The passage reminds us that heaven is not indifferent to suffering. The Lord sees, hears, and responds.

---

### Compassion in Financial Dealings
> “You shall not charge him interest."

God’s instruction concerning lending reveals another dimension of biblical justice. The poor should not become an opportunity for profit. The purpose of lending within the covenant community was restoration, not exploitation.

**Modern Relevance:** While economies differ, the principle remains: God’s people should seek the welfare of others rather than taking advantage of their hardship.

#### Reflection
- Am I seeking personal gain at the expense of others?
- How can I help rather than profit from someone’s need?

---

### Jesus: The Perfect Fulfillment

Jesus embodied every principle found in Exodus 22:21‑25. He welcomed outsiders, touched lepers, defended the vulnerable, cared for widows, blessed children, fed the hungry, and preached good news to the poor. Most importantly, He became the ultimate expression of God’s mercy. Though humanity was spiritually poor and unable to save itself, Christ paid a debt we could never repay.

---

### Application for Today
As followers of Christ, we are called to:
- Welcome strangers with love and dignity.
- Protect and support vulnerable individuals.
- Listen to those who are hurting.
- Practice generosity rather than exploitation.
- Reflect God’s justice and mercy in everyday life.

Christian maturity is measured not only by knowledge of Scripture but also by the extent to which God’s compassion is visible in our lives.

---

### Discussion Questions
1. Why did God remind Israel that they had once been strangers in Egypt?
2. What does this passage teach us about God’s character?
3. Why does God place special emphasis on widows and orphans?
4. How can believers today apply these commands in their communities?
5. What role does generosity play in reflecting God’s heart?
6. How does Jesus fulfill and exemplify the principles found in Exodus 22:21‑25?
